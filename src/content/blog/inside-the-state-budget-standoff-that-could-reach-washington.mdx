When the legislature in Columbus adjourned on Thursday without a transit budget, it did more than delay bus service expansions in three counties. It handed Washington a test case. Two other states — Michigan and Pennsylvania — are stuck on nearly identical bills, and all three are waiting on the same pool of federal matching money.

The dispute is narrow on paper. Each state must commit a local share before it can draw down federal infrastructure grants due to expire next September. In practice, the fight has become a proxy for a broader argument about who should pay to maintain systems built with federal dollars a generation ago.

## What is actually at stake

According to budget documents reviewed by Classic Reporters, roughly $1.8 billion in federal matching funds across the three states depends on local commitments being signed before March. State transportation officials confirmed the figure in interviews this week.

* Ohio’s share covers bus fleet replacement in Franklin, Cuyahoga, and Hamilton counties.
* Michigan’s portion is tied to a rail maintenance backlog first reported in 2024.
* Pennsylvania’s bill bundles transit with road repair, which complicates the vote count.

> “Nobody wants to be the state that left federal money on the table. But nobody wants to be the one that raised fares to get it, either.” — Dana Whitmore, Ohio Senate Transportation Committee

## Why Washington is watching

Federal officials have said little publicly. A Department of Transportation spokesperson said in a statement that the agency “continues to work with state partners” and declined to comment on whether deadlines could be extended. Two congressional aides, speaking on background because they were not authorized to discuss negotiations, said an extension is under discussion but not guaranteed.

![A transit depot awaiting fleet replacement funds.](/classic-assets/6892f3c0e83829a3.jpg)

## What comes next

The Ohio legislature is scheduled to reconvene on October 20. Michigan’s House has a hearing set for the following week. If neither state moves before Thanksgiving, transportation officials say, contracting timelines make the March deadline difficult to meet.
